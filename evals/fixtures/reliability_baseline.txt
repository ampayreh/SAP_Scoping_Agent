## 2.1 Scoring Methodology

Each module is evaluated across five dimensions:
- **Business Relevance** (0–10): How critical is this functional area to CSS operations?
- **Current Pain Level** (0–10): Severity of documented pain points in this area
- **S/4HANA Uplift** (0–10): Degree of improvement S/4HANA delivers vs. ECC
- **Implementation Risk** (0–10, lower = higher risk): Complexity and risk of implementation
- **Strategic Alignment** (0–10): Fit with CSS strategic drivers and parent ecosystem

**Overall Fit Score** = Weighted composite (0–100)

## 2.3 Critical Module Deep-Dives

### 🔴 GTS — Global Trade Services (Fit Score: 90 | MANDATORY PHASE 1)

**Rationale:** ITAR/EAR compliance for a satellite manufacturer is not a best-practice recommendation — it is a legal requirement. The current state (partial GTS + manual processes + separate access control database) represents **unacceptable compliance risk**. Any satellite component exported, any foreign national employee accessing controlled technical data, and any launch service transaction involves ITAR applicability. The consequences of non-compliance include criminal penalties, debarment from US government contracts, and export privilege revocation.

**S/4HANA GTS Capabilities Needed:**
- Export License Management (ITAR/EAR license tracking and automated holds)
- Restricted Party Screening (OFAC, BIS, State Dept lists — automated with SAP Watch List Screening)
- Embargo and Sanctions Management
- Electronic Commerce Control List (ECCN) classification management
- Deemed Export controls for foreign national employee access
- Integration with Teamcenter PLM for controlled technical data classification

**Implementation Considerations:** GTS in S/4HANA is significantly enhanced vs. ECC GTS. The partial ECC GTS implementation will require a full gap assessment. Recommend engaging a GTS specialist subcontractor with ITAR/EAR domain expertise alongside the core SI.

---

### ✅ RAR — Revenue Accounting & Reporting (Fit Score: 88 | MANDATORY PHASE 1)

**Rationale:** CSS's business model — long-term satellite manufacturing contracts with milestone-based payments — is precisely the scenario IFRS 15/ASC 606 was designed to govern. Current FI configuration cannot handle:
- Performance obligation identification and allocation across satellite contracts
- Variable consideration (launch success milestones, on-orbit performance)
- Percentage-of-completion revenue recognition for multi-year programs
- Contract modification accounting

**S/4HANA RAR Capabilities:**
- Contract Management with POB (Performance Obligation) decomposition
- Multiple revenue recognition methods: PoC, milestone, time-based
- Integration with PS (Project System) for contract-driven milestone billing
- Dual-book accounting (IFRS 15 recognition vs. cash billing)
- Disclosure reports for IFRS 15 Note disclosures

**Risk Note:** RAR is technically complex and organizationally impactful (requires Finance + Legal + Program Management alignment). Plan for extended UAT and parallel-run period.

---

### ✅ BTP — Business Technology Platform (Fit Score: 86 | CROSS-PHASE FOUNDATION)

**Rationale:** BTP is the enabler for the CIO's Clean Core mandate. With 2,400 custom objects, the target is not to eliminate all extensions but to **move them to SAP-endorsed extensibility patterns** on BTP. This is what makes Clean Core achievable without eliminating business-critical functionality.

**BTP Services for CSS:**
- **SAP Integration Suite:** Replace 12 custom middleware interfaces with managed, monitored integration flows. Critical for Teamcenter ↔ S/4HANA BOM sync and MES near-real-time integration
- **SAP Build / ABAP Cloud:** Redevelop custom transactions as BTP-native apps or ABAP Cloud extensions
- **SAP Datasphere + SAC:** Analytics and reporting layer replacing custom ABAP reports
- **SAP AI Business Services:** Foundation for CEO's AI-assisted supply chain planning vision

---

### ✅ IBP — Integrated Business Planning (Fit Score: 88 | PHASE 2)

**Rationale:** 18+ month lead times for space-grade electronics, combined with a 3x production rate increase, make sophisticated supply chain planning existential — not optional. Current MRP in ECC has no predictive or probabilistic capabilities.

**IBP Capabilities for CSS:**
- **IBP for Supply:** Constrained network planning with 18-24 month planning horizons
- **IBP for Demand:** Satellite delivery schedule → component demand decomposition
- **IBP for Response & Supply:** Supply disruption sensing and response
- **Supply Chain Control Tower:** Real-time visibility across CSS's supply network
- Integration with Anaplan: IBP and Anaplan have overlapping financial planning capabilities — a rationalization assessment is recommended (consider retiring Anaplan in Phase 3)

# SECTION 3: IMPLEMENTATION ROADMAP

## 3.1 Approach Recommendation: System Conversion (Brownfield)

**Recommendation: System Conversion (Brownfield) to SAP S/4HANA Cloud Private Edition**

**Rationale:**

| Factor | Greenfield | Brownfield (Recommended) | Selective Data Migration |
|---|---|---|---|
| Historical data preservation | ❌ Loss of 10 years ECC history | ✅ Full history preserved | 🔶 Partial |
| Custom code handling | Clean slate | Explicit remediation program | Hybrid |
| Implementation risk | Very High (process redesign at scale) | High (managed conversion) | Very High (two parallel systems) |
| Timeline to 2028 | IMPOSSIBLE at CSS scale | Achievable with phasing | High risk |
| Cost vs. $15-25M budget | Exceeds budget | Fits with discipline | Likely exceeds budget |
| Business disruption | Extreme | Managed | Extreme |
| ECC investment preservation | Lost | ✅ Preserved | Partial |
| Parent alignment (PE model) | Possible | ✅ Direct path | Possible |

**Why not Greenfield?** With 800 users, 9 active modules, 2,400 custom objects, 6 integrated systems, and a 2028 hard deadline, a full Greenfield implementation is not achievable within the timeline or budget constraints. The organizational change fatigue from two failed projects makes a "rip and replace" approach politically untenable.

**Why not Selective Data Migration?** Two projects in three years have failed. A third approach requiring operation of parallel ECC and S/4HANA systems simultaneously would collapse under organizational fatigue.

**Brownfield with Clean Core Discipline:** The conversion preserves the ECC configuration investment, data history, and organizational familiarity while systematically addressing custom code through a parallel remediation workstream. This is the risk-balanced approach for CSS.

**Deployment Model: SAP S/4HANA Cloud Private Edition (hosted)**
- Aligns with parent conglomerate's existing PE deployment
- US-based data center option available (ITAR data sovereignty compliance)
- Eliminates on-premise infrastructure burden from 4-FTE Basis team
- Enables parent-driven innovation cycle (quarterly feature releases)
- RISE with SAP framework provides commercialization simplicity

### THE SITUATION: TWO CLOCKS ARE TICKING

Constellation Satellite Systems faces a simultaneous strategic opportunity and technical imperative that converge on the same deadline.

**The business opportunity** is substantial: scaling from 5 to 15 satellites per month represents a 3x growth in production complexity — more components, more programs, more regulatory scrutiny, more engineering changes, more financial intricacy. Your current ECC system, heavily customized and operationally constrained, cannot scale to support this trajectory. Every month of delayed modernization increases the operational risk of your production rate targets.

**The technical imperative** is non-negotiable: SAP ECC mainstream maintenance ends in 2027, with extended maintenance concluding in 2030. Your Board has set December 2028 as the transformation deadline — a prudent decision that provides a 24-month buffer before the absolute end of supportability. That buffer is not large. We are recommending program initiation in Q2 2025. Any later start materially increases program risk.

These two clocks — business growth and technical sunset — make this transformation both urgent and strategically vital. The question is not *whether* to transform, but *how* to do it in a way that succeeds where the organization has recently been disappointed.

---

### THE RECOMMENDATION IN BRIEF

We recommend a **System Conversion (Brownfield) to SAP S/4HANA Cloud Private Edition**, implemented in a **phased, multi-wave approach** over **30 months**, targeting a Q3 2028 go-live — three months ahead of the Board deadline to provide stability assurance.

This is not a rip-and-replace. It preserves ten years of business logic, configuration investment, and institutional knowledge embedded in your ECC system, while systematically eliminating the technical debt, manual workarounds, and compliance gaps that are constraining CSS today.

---

### WHY BROWNFIELD — AND WHY SAP S/4HANA CLOUD PRIVATE EDITION

**System Conversion is the right approach for CSS for three reasons:**

First, it is the only approach that can meet your timeline. A Greenfield reimplementation of 9 modules, 800 users, and 6 major integrations — including full GTS and RAR — cannot be responsibly executed to a 2028 deadline at CSS's scale and within the allocated budget.

Second, it is the only approach your organization can absorb right now. Two failed IT projects in three years have created real organizational skepticism. A conversion — which works *with* existing processes rather than *against* them — has a fundamentally lower organizational disruption profile. The workforce recognizes the system. Process changes are introduced deliberately and explicitly, not as a byproduct of a wholesale reimplementation.

Third, it preserves the investment and knowledge in your current system while creating the foundation for the Clean Core, BTP-extensible architecture that your CIO correctly identifies as the target state.

**SAP S/4HANA Cloud Private Edition** is the correct deployment model because:
- It directly aligns with your parent conglomerate's existing PE footprint — reducing future integration complexity and positioning CSS for consolidated operational and reporting benefits
- It provides US-based data center options, satisfying ITAR data sovereignty requirements that an on-premise alternative would address less efficiently
- It transfers infrastructure operational burden from your 4-person Basis team (which is undersized for the current environment and certainly undersized for a transformation) to SAP's managed services
- It positions CSS on the SAP quarterly innovation release cycle — meaning the AI and digital factory capabilities your CEO envisions will continue to arrive as standard functionality, rather than requiring custom development

### THE FOUR NON-NEGOTIABLE PRIORITIES

Within the broader scope, four items are non-negotiable. These are not scope preferences — they are business and legal imperatives.

**1. GTS / ITAR Compliance — Phase 1, No Exceptions**

Your current ITAR compliance posture — partial GTS, manual processes, and a separate access control database — is an unacceptable risk profile for a satellite manufacturer. A single ITAR violation can result in criminal penalties, debarment from US government contracts, and revocation of export privileges. Your CISO is correct to treat this as a priority, and we are elevating it to a non-negotiable Phase 1 deliverable. Full GTS implementation, including automated restricted party screening, export license management, deemed export controls, and ECCN classification, will be live before Phase 2 begins.

We recommend immediately sourcing a specialized GTS subcontractor with ITAR-cleared resources. This talent pool is constrained, and early contracting is essential.

**2. Revenue Recognition (RAR / IFRS 15 / ASC 606) — Phase 1**

Your long-term satellite contracts require revenue recognition capabilities that your current FI configuration does not support. This is both a financial reporting risk and an investor/board credibility issue. SAP Revenue Accounting and Reporting (RAR), fully integrated with Project System, will provide the milestone-based, performance-obligation-compliant revenue recognition your CFO requires. The 5-day close target is achievable — but only with the Universal Journal and proper RAR configuration working in concert.

**3. Integrated BOM Management — Phase 2 Critical Workstream**

The absence of a reliable EBOM-to-MBOM-to-As-Built BOM chain is not a process inconvenience — it is a fundamental constraint on quality, traceability, and rate scalability. At 5 satellites per month, manual as-built BOM tracking is painful. At 15 per month, it is impossible. The BOM integration architecture — connecting Teamcenter (EBOM master), S/4HANA (MBOM master), and a digital as-built tracking capability — is the most technically complex workstream in Phase 2 and requires dedicated specialist resources including Siemens professional services.

**4. Clean Core Discipline — Entire Program**

With 2,400 custom ABAP objects, CSS carries one of the heaviest customization burdens we encounter in mid-size transformations. The CIO's 60% reduction target is achievable — but it requires active governance throughout the program, not a post-implementation cleanup effort. We will conduct a full Custom Code Migration Tool (CCMT) analysis in the Pre-Phase, producing a disposition register for every object. Roughly 35% of objects are likely candidates for immediate retirement (replaced by standard S/4HANA functionality or no longer used). Another 40% will be remediated to SAP BTP-compliant extensions. Only the irreducible specialized logic — estimated at 10% — will remain as documented technical debt with a defined deprecation roadmap.

### ADDRESSING THE ORGANIZATIONAL REALITY

We must be direct about something that other proposals may not address: the organizational context of this transformation is as important as its technical design.

Two IT project failures in three years have consequences beyond the projects themselves. They have created warranted skepticism about large-scale IT programs across CSS. Business leaders have been burned. Users have been asked to change how they work, disrupted in that work, and then seen the initiative fail. That experience does not disappear because this program has a larger budget and a more experienced team.

We are proposing the following specific structural responses to this reality:

**Co-Sponsorship Model.** This program requires CEO and CFO as co-executive sponsors — not delegated to CIO or VP-level. Visible executive commitment, demonstrated through regular all-hands program communications and Steering Committee presence, is the single most important change fatigue mitigation available.

**Early Wins Before the Hard Work.** In the Pre-Phase, we will deploy SAP Signavio to generate immediate process insights from your existing ECC system. Business stakeholders will receive process mining dashboards, waste identification reports, and automation opportunity analyses within 90 days of program start — before a single line of S/4HANA configuration is written. This demonstrates program value early, builds credibility, and generates the data needed to make smart custom code retirement decisions.

**Independent OCM Practice.** We are recommending that Organizational Change Management be staffed as an independent workstream with its own budget and leadership — not embedded within the SI engagement. The OCM lead should report directly to the CSS Program Director, not to the SI project manager. OCM is not a training project. It is the organizational transformation that makes the technical transformation successful.

**Phased Go-Live Reduces Blast Radius.** Phase 1 (Finance + GTS) affects primarily the Finance and Trade Compliance organizations — approximately 150–200 users. Manufacturing, Supply Chain, and Operations users will see process improvements from the integration upgrades, but their core systems do not change in Phase 1. This staged approach means the program can demonstrate a successful Phase 1 go-live — a concrete, visible win — before asking the manufacturing organization to change how it works in Phase 2.

**Honest Communication About What This Is.** This program is larger, more complex, and more consequential than the two prior failed projects. We will not hide that. We will, however, run it with a transparency model — real-time program dashboards available to the Steering Committee, monthly status reports to the full leadership team, and a risk escalation process with teeth — that earns trust through honesty rather than optimism.
