The evidence is visible today: inventory discrepancies that cannot be traced, revenue recognition handled through manual workarounds, export compliance managed across three disconnected systems, and a financial close that consumes twelve business days every month. ## 2. Current State Assessment

### Business Context and Strategic Position

CSS operates in an industry where technical precision, regulatory compliance, and supply chain reliability are not differentiators — they are entry requirements. Every satellite that rolls off the Redmond or Huntsville line represents a complex convergence of hundreds of space-grade components with 18-plus month lead times, specialized manufacturing processes subject to AS9100 quality standards, ITAR-controlled technology requiring export compliance at every transaction, and long-term contract obligations that must be recognized as revenue in accordance with IFRS 15 and ASC 606.

Your production ramp ambition — tripling output within eighteen months — compresses all of that complexity into a much shorter cycle and amplifies every operational gap that currently exists.

### Operational Challenges

The pain points CSS has identified fall into three interconnected clusters. Understanding how they connect is important, because addressing them in isolation produces partial solutions.

**The Compliance Cluster.** ITAR compliance is currently managed across three systems: a partial SAP Global Trade Services implementation, manual processes, and a separate access control database. Your CISO has identified gaps. In a satellite manufacturing environment — where every product is ITAR Category XV controlled — this is not a technology gap to be scheduled for remediation. It is an active compliance exposure that, if triggered, can result in debarment from US government contracts, loss of export licenses, and criminal liability for individuals. The fragmentation of controls across three disconnected systems means that a transaction can slip through without the required checks. This must be the first thing fixed.

Revenue recognition sits in the same cluster. CSS's long-term satellite contracts require milestone-based or percentage-of-completion revenue recognition under IFRS 15 and ASC 606. Your current SAP Financial Accounting configuration does not support this natively — the recognition is performed through manual workarounds. For a company reporting $1.2 billion in annual revenue as a subsidiary of a publicly traded conglomerate, manual revenue recognition is a Sarbanes-Oxley control gap, not just an operational inconvenience. Every quarterly close carries the risk that the workarounds produce a number that cannot withstand audit scrutiny.

**The Manufacturing Intelligence Cluster.** Your engineering bill-of-materials lives in Siemens Teamcenter. Your manufacturing bill-of-materials lives in SAP Production Planning. The synchronization between them is broken. Your as-built bill-of-materials — the record of what actually went into each satellite — is tracked manually with no system of record. For a manufacturer producing complex, serialized, high-value aerospace hardware at scale, this is the equivalent of building without blueprints and recording the results on paper.

The Apriso manufacturing execution system does communicate with SAP, but on a four-hour lag. Production supervisors are making real-time decisions based on data that is, at minimum, four hours stale. As production volume triples, the operational cost of that lag triples with it.

Earned value management for satellite program tracking is performed in Excel, manually extracted from SAP Project System. The result is that program cost and schedule performance data is never current, always dependent on an individual who knows how to build the extract, and unavailable in real time to the people who need it.

**The Data Fragmentation Cluster.** Underpinning all of the above is a fundamental architectural problem: CSS's operational data is fragmented across systems that do not integrate cleanly. This is not the result of poor decisions — it is the natural consequence of a heavily customized ECC landscape that has accumulated 2,400 custom code objects and 47 custom transactions over a decade, plus twelve custom-built middleware interfaces that connect systems without a modern integration architecture. The result is a data environment where nothing is trusted as the single source of truth, reconciliation is manual, and the systems that should enable faster decisions instead create the need for more manual work.

### The Risk of Standing Still

CSS has until 2030 before SAP ends extended maintenance for ECC 6.0. That date feels distant from a February 2026 vantage point. It is not. Consider what the next four years look like without transformation:

- The production ramp to fifteen satellites per month proceeds on systems designed for five. Every operational gap described above scales proportionally — or worse, non-linearly.
- The extended maintenance premium SAP charges post-2027 adds cost without adding capability. You will pay more for a system that falls further behind.
- The compliance exposure grows with transaction volume. More satellites shipped means more ITAR-controlled transactions passing through a fragmented control environment.
- The gap between CSS's enterprise systems and the parent conglomerate's S/4HANA Cloud Private Edition platform widens, making group consolidation, data sharing, and architecture alignment progressively harder to achieve.
- Competitors who have already completed their S/4HANA migrations are beginning to deploy embedded AI capabilities — predictive quality, AI-assisted supply chain planning, real-time financial intelligence — that are architecturally unavailable to CSS while it operates on a customized ECC landscape.

The cost of inaction is not zero. It is the sum of escalating maintenance costs, compliance risk premiums, competitive capability gaps, and the organizational cost of continuing to perform manually what technology should handle automatically.

## 3. Proposed Solution

### Strategic Fit: Why S/4HANA Cloud Private Edition

The recommended platform is SAP S/4HANA Cloud Private Edition, delivered via SAP's GROW or RISE with SAP commercial model, hosted on US-based data centers with documented ITAR data residency compliance.

This is the right choice for CSS for four specific reasons that have nothing to do with parent company pressure:

First, the configuration depth that CSS requires — full GTS for ITAR/EAR compliance, Revenue Accounting and Reporting for IFRS 15 and ASC 606, complex as-built bill-of-materials management, Earned Value Management in Project System — is simply not available in the Public Cloud edition of S/4HANA. Private Edition provides cloud delivery (SAP manages the infrastructure, applies updates, and ensures availability) while preserving the configuration flexibility that an aerospace and defense manufacturer of CSS's complexity requires.

Second, S/4HANA Cloud Private Edition is the platform your parent conglomerate has already deployed. Alignment is not just a corporate governance requirement — it is a practical advantage. Group consolidation, intercompany transactions, shared reporting standards, and access to parent-level SAP licenses and capabilities all become significantly simpler when both entities operate on the same technical foundation.

Third, ITAR data residency requirements constrain CSS's cloud options in ways that favor a large, certified cloud host over a generic hosting arrangement. SAP's Private Edition hosting on US Azure or AWS data centers, with documented compliance certifications, is the clearest path to satisfying the requirement that export-controlled technical data and transaction records remain within US jurisdiction. This must be confirmed contractually with SAP before the cloud agreement is signed.

Fourth, the Clean Core journey that your CIO has rightly mandated — reducing 2,400 custom code objects and eliminating in-system modifications — is only achievable sustainably on a platform designed for clean extension. S/4HANA Cloud Private Edition, combined with SAP Business Technology Platform as the extension and integration layer, provides exactly this architecture.

### Scope Summary

The proposed scope covers seven core business areas and two foundational platform capabilities, organized into the three delivery waves described in Section 4.

**Financial Management and Controlling**

CSS will replace the current ECC Financial Accounting and Controlling configuration with S/4HANA's Universal Journal architecture — a single ledger that eliminates the reconciliation overhead between FI and CO that contributes to your twelve-day close. Real-time financial analytics, automated intercompany reconciliation, and parallel accounting for IFRS and US GAAP will all be delivered within this scope. Program-level profitability analysis — giving your CFO the ability to see margin by satellite program, customer, and product line in real time — will be configured in the Management Accounting module. Group Reporting integration will align CSS's financial close with parent conglomerate consolidation requirements.

The financial close target of five business days is achievable with this configuration. Industry benchmarks for S/4HANA implementations in aerospace manufacturing companies of comparable size show median close cycles of three to five days, compared to an ECC baseline typically in the ten-to-fourteen day range.

**Revenue Accounting and Reporting (IFRS 15 / ASC 606)**

SAP's Revenue Accounting and Reporting module — the native S/4HANA solution for contract-based revenue recognition — will be implemented as a dedicated workstream within Wave 1. This is not a configuration item. For a company with CSS's contract portfolio — long-term satellite production contracts with milestone billing, performance obligations spanning multiple fiscal years, and revenue recognition that must satisfy both IFRS 15 and ASC 606 simultaneously — this is a substantial implementation requiring close collaboration between your Finance, Legal, and Revenue teams to define the contract performance obligation structure before a single configuration parameter is set.

The outcome: automated, auditable revenue recognition that eliminates the manual workarounds currently in place and closes the SOX control gap in your revenue reporting.

**Export Compliance and Global Trade Management**

The partial GTS implementation will be completed and extended to full deployment, covering every capability your CISO requires: license determination and tracking for all ITAR and EAR-controlled items, embargo checks on every relevant transaction, denied party screening on all business partners, end-use control for dual-use goods under the Commerce Control List, and a complete audit trail for all export-controlled transactions.

The architectural difference between the current state and the target state is critical: today, export compliance controls are a combination of system prompts, manual process steps, and a separate access control database — meaning a determined or distracted user can bypass them. In the target state, GTS-enforced controls are embedded in the transaction flow in SAP. A procurement order, sales order, or goods movement touching an ITAR-controlled item cannot be processed without passing the required GTS checks. The control is systemic, not procedural.

**Procurement and Supply Chain Management**

The Materials Management scope will be upgraded to S/4HANA's enhanced procurement and inventory management capabilities, with particular focus on the long-lead component challenge. Space-grade electronics components with 18-plus month lead times currently have no predictive tracking in SAP — supply disruptions are discovered reactively. The Wave 1 scope will establish the data foundation (supplier master data quality, material classification, lead-time documentation) required to implement SAP Integrated Business Planning as a Wave 3 capability. Wave 3 will deliver AI-assisted demand sensing and supplier delivery risk scoring to give your Supply Chain team the predictive visibility they need.

**Project Management and Earned Value**

SAP Project System, upgraded to S/4HANA's project management capabilities, will include the Earned Value Management configuration that eliminates the current Excel-based EVM process. Real-time cost performance index and schedule performance index data, milestone billing integration with Revenue Accounting, and program-level financial dashboards will give your Program Managers and CFO the program visibility they need without a spreadsheet in the chain. If DCAA and DFARS government contract accounting requirements apply — a question that must be confirmed before scope finalization — the configuration will include the additional cost accounting structure those standards require.

**Production Planning and BOM Management**

Wave 2 will deliver the integrated bill-of-materials capability that your VP Manufacturing has identified as the most operationally significant gap. The scope covers three interdependent components: redesign of the SAP Integration Suite connector between Siemens Teamcenter and S/4HANA to establish reliable, bidirectional engineering-to-manufacturing BOM synchronization; configuration of S/4HANA Production Planning for the serialized, batch-managed manufacturing environment of satellite production; and as-built BOM capture from the Apriso MES system, eliminating the current manual recording process.

This scope is deliberately positioned in Wave 2 rather than Wave 1, for reasons explained in Section 4. The BOM data remediation required before this capability can go live — resolving the years of divergence between engineering and manufacturing records — is a business process effort that must run in parallel with Wave 1 and be completed before Wave 2 go-live. It cannot be rushed.

**Quality Management and Plant Maintenance**

AS9100-aligned digital quality management, replacing paper-based quality records with electronic nonconformance management, lot traceability, and quality inspection recording integrated with the production order. Plant Maintenance, covering manufacturing equipment at Redmond and Huntsville and launch facility equipment at Cape Canaveral, will be upgraded with the S/4HANA maintenance order and equipment master capabilities. Predictive quality through embedded AI analysis of quality inspection data is a Wave 3 capability that becomes available once the digital quality record foundation is in place.

**Platform: SAP Business Technology Platform**

BTP is not a module — it is the architectural foundation that makes Clean Core possible. Every remaining custom business logic that cannot be replaced by S/4HANA standard functionality will be rebuilt as a BTP extension using the ABAP Cloud or Rapid Application Programming model, ensuring it remains compatible with S/4HANA updates. All twelve custom middleware interfaces will be migrated to SAP Integration Suite standard connectors or custom integration flows on BTP. The result is an integration architecture that is visible, governed, and maintainable — rather than the current collection of individually built, individually fragile point-to-point connections.

The mobile money payment integration analogy from other industries applies here too: your Teamcenter PLM connector, your Apriso MES integration, your Salesforce CRM interface, and your Workday HR connection will all operate through a single, managed integration platform rather than through bespoke code that only the original developer understands.

**The Clean Core Journey**

Your CIO's target of reducing custom code by 60% — from approximately 2,400 objects to approximately 960 — is the right directional goal for this phase. It is important to frame it accurately: 60% reduction is a milestone, not the destination. S/4HANA Cloud Private Edition's Clean Core standard ultimately requires zero in-system modifications. The remaining objects after Phase 1 remediation must be migrated to BTP or eliminated in subsequent phases.

The path to 60% reduction follows a structured sequence. First, the SAP Readiness Check and Custom Code Analytics tool — available through SAP Cloud ALM — will classify all 2,400 objects by usage activity in the last twelve months. Industry experience with ECC landscapes of this age suggests that 30 to 50 percent of objects have not been executed in the last year and can be safely retired. Second, objects where S/4HANA now provides native functionality that CSS customized ECC to achieve will be replaced with standard configuration. Third, the remaining business-critical custom logic will be rebuilt on BTP. This work begins immediately, runs as a dedicated workstream throughout the program, and is not a sequential step that starts after the technical conversion.

### Integration Architecture Overview

The target integration architecture replaces twelve individually built, point-to-point middleware connections with a single, SAP Integration Suite-governed hub-and-spoke model. The practical implication for CSS's business: when Salesforce creates a contract, it arrives in S/4HANA through a managed, monitored interface. When Teamcenter releases an engineering BOM change, it flows to S/4HANA Production Planning through a governed connector with error handling and reconciliation. When Apriso records a production confirmation, it reaches S/4HANA in near-real-time rather than on a four-hour batch cycle.

The integration governance responsibility shifts from ad hoc maintenance by whoever built the original interface to a managed platform with monitoring dashboards, error alerting, and version control. This is what Clean Core integration looks like in practice.

SAP Signavio will serve as the process intelligence layer, providing both the parent-required process documentation and the baseline mapping of current ECC process flows to S/4HANA standard processes. SAP LeanIX will document the application landscape rationalization decisions — which of the twelve interfaces get replaced, which systems get retained, which are candidates for future consolidation. Both tools are recommended for immediate adoption, before the formal transformation program begins.

## 4. Implementation Approach

### Methodology

The program will follow SAP Activate, SAP's standard implementation methodology organized into five phases: Discover (scoping and business case), Prepare (program setup), Explore (requirements confirmation and gap analysis), Realize (configuration and build), and Deploy (testing, training, and go-live).

In practical terms: the program begins with structured workshops to confirm exactly what the system needs to do for CSS's specific business processes. The system is then configured to match those requirements, tested thoroughly by CSS business users against real operational scenarios, and deployed with full on-site support through the first complete operating cycle.

### Three-Wave Delivery Strategy

A single go-live covering all scope simultaneously is not feasible within the budget envelope, the thirty-four-month timeline to the December 2028 board deadline, or the organizational capacity of a company simultaneously tripling its production rate. A phased, three-wave approach is the only architecture that delivers the board mandate reliably.

**Wave 1 — Foundation: Finance, Compliance, and Program Management (Target: Q4 2027, approximately 22 months)**

Wave 1 delivers the highest-priority, highest-risk items: the capabilities where CSS has active compliance exposure today and where the CFO and board have the clearest visibility requirements.

| Capability | Business Outcome |
|---|---|
| S/4HANA Financial Accounting and Controlling | Five-day financial close; real-time program profitability; automated multi-currency processing |
| Revenue Accounting and Reporting | Automated IFRS 15 and ASC 606 recognition; SOX control gap closed; audit-ready revenue records |
| Full Global Trade Services (GTS) | ITAR and EAR controls enforced at the transaction level; compliance gap remediated; CISO objective met |
| Project System with Earned Value Management | Excel EVM eliminated; real-time cost and schedule performance; milestone billing automated |
| Procurement and Inventory Management (Finance integration) | Integrated procure-to-pay; open PO and inventory data on S/4HANA |
| SAP Integration Suite: Salesforce, Workday, Anaplan connectors | Three custom middleware interfaces replaced; integration architecture modernized |
| Custom code Wave 1 remediation | Unused objects retired; standard-covered customizations replaced; estimated 40-50% reduction in object count |
| SAP Signavio process baseline | Current-state process documentation; gap analysis against S/4HANA standard; parent adoption completed |
| SAP Cloud ALM program governance | Project management, custom code analytics, test management, change tracking |

Wave 1 does not include production planning or shop floor scope. This is intentional. The manufacturing organization will be operating at or near peak stress during the production ramp to fifteen satellites per month, which peaks in the same timeframe. Asking manufacturing teams to absorb a system change during that ramp is the scenario most likely to produce the organizational failure mode that characterizes CSS's two prior IT project failures. Wave 1 delivers visible wins to Finance, Compliance, and Program Management while the manufacturing organization stabilizes.

**Wave 2 — Manufacturing and BOM Integration (Target: Q3 2028, approximately 31 months)**

Wave 2 delivers the manufacturing capabilities your VP Manufacturing has identified as the central operational gap. The nine-month gap between Wave 1 and Wave 2 go-live is not wasted time — it is when the BOM data remediation workstream, which must begin in Wave 1, produces the clean engineering and manufacturing BOM data that Wave 2 requires to function correctly.

Wave 2 go-live in Q3 2028 delivers the full core scope before the December 2028 board deadline with a three-month buffer — time that will be used for Wave 2 stabilization and hypercare rather than as schedule float.

**Wave 3 — Advanced Capabilities and Digital Factory (Target: 2029)**

Wave 3 delivers the CEO's digital factory vision. These capabilities are genuinely transformative but require the Wave 1 and Wave 2 data foundation to function. ### Go-Live Strategy

Each wave uses a regional or functional cutover approach rather than a single-moment big-bang go-live. Wave 1 finance go-live will be coordinated across Redmond, Huntsville, and Cape Canaveral on a single financial closing date, with a parallel running period for critical financial reconciliation processes. Wave 2 manufacturing go-live will use a controlled production-line cutover approach — validated on a single production cell before full-site rollout — given the operational risk of disrupting satellite production.

The go-live strategy for the Teamcenter and Apriso integrations (Wave 2) will include a mandatory parallel running period of at least four weeks, where both the old integration and the new S/4HANA integration operate simultaneously with daily reconciliation. This is a direct lesson from the prior failed PLM and MES projects: integration failures discovered in production, rather than in testing, are the most operationally destructive failure mode.

### Change Management

The organizational dimension of this program deserves candor that is not always present in technology proposals. CSS is asking 800 people to adopt a system that works differently from the one they have used for ten years — at the same time it is asking the manufacturing organization to triple production output — in an organizational environment that has watched two large IT projects fail recently.

The change management approach is designed for that reality, not for an idealized one.

The first step is a published post-mortem of the two failed projects. Before the organization is asked to trust again, leadership must acknowledge what went wrong — specifically and transparently. A post-mortem conducted with key stakeholders and shared internally is the most powerful trust-building action available before the S/4HANA program begins. It signals that this time, the organization has learned, and it invites the people who lived through the failures to contribute to a program design that avoids repeating them.

The second element is business-owned design. The target business processes for S/4HANA will be designed by CSS business process owners — Finance, Manufacturing, Export Compliance, Program Management — with implementation partner support. They are not handed to them by IT at the end of a configuration phase. The distinction matters: people adopt processes they designed, and resist processes that were done to them.

The third element is role-specific training that reflects actual job duties. A production planner's training will cover the production planning transactions they use daily. A program manager's training will cover Earned Value Management and project reporting. An export compliance coordinator's training will cover the GTS license management and denied party screening workflows. No one sits through a generic system overview that covers modules they will never touch.

The fourth element is visible early wins. SAP Signavio process baseline workshops, initiated immediately and before the formal program begins, give the organization a tangible deliverable within sixty days: documented current-state processes and a comparison against S/4HANA standard, making the design decisions visible and participatory rather than mysterious.

### Quality Assurance

Four safeguards are non-negotiable:

1. A formal Go/No-Go gate at the end of each Realize phase, with documented criteria: custom code remediation completion percentage, integration test pass rate, data migration accuracy, user training completion, and hypercare resourcing confirmed on-site.
2. Two full data migration rehearsals for each wave before the production migration. Every data error found in rehearsal is one that does not appear on the first live transaction.
3. End-to-end process testing executed by CSS business users — not implementation consultants — against the full library of expected transaction scenarios. A test suite that consultants pass but users fail is not a passing test suite.
4. A dedicated production support team physically present at each CSS site for the first full operating cycle post-go-live: through the first monthly financial close (Wave 1) and through the first complete satellite production order cycle (Wave 2).

### Return on Investment

The ROI case for this program rests on four quantifiable pillars and one strategic one.

**Financial close acceleration.** Moving from a twelve-day to a five-day close frees approximately seven business days per month for the Finance organization — time currently consumed by manual reconciliation, spreadsheet consolidation, and variance investigation. For a Finance team supporting $1.2 billion in revenue, industry benchmarks suggest this translates to 3 to 5 FTE equivalents of capacity redirected from close mechanics to financial analysis and business partnering. At a fully loaded cost of $150,000 to $200,000 per FTE, the annual labor value is approximately $450,000 to $1,000,000 — recurring.

**Export compliance risk elimination.** Quantifying the value of not receiving an ITAR violation notice is inherently imprecise, but the reference data is sobering. Recent ITAR enforcement actions against aerospace and defense companies have resulted in settlements ranging from $13 million to $30 million, plus the reputational and contractual consequences of a State Department compliance agreement. Closing the identified ITAR compliance gaps through full GTS implementation eliminates an exposure that is not currently reflected on CSS's balance sheet but is materially present in its operating environment.

**Revenue recognition automation.** The current manual revenue recognition process for long-term contracts carries both a labor cost and an audit risk. The labor cost — estimated at 2 to 4 FTE equivalents in Finance and Revenue, based on the complexity described — represents $300,000 to $800,000 annually. The audit risk, for a SOX-reporting subsidiary of a public company, is harder to quantify but includes the cost of external audit remediation, potential restatement work, and the ongoing cost of maintaining workarounds that every new Finance hire must be trained to execute.

**Supply chain disruption prevention.** When the Wave 3 SAP Integrated Business Planning capability is in place, predictive tracking of 18-plus month lead-time components provides advance warning of supply disruptions rather than reactive discovery. At fifteen satellites per month with a fully deployed constellation schedule, a single unplanned component shortage that delays one month of production represents lost revenue in the range of $40 million to $80 million, based on satellite contract values at current market pricing. IBP's value is measured in the disruptions it prevents, which by definition does not appear in a line item — but the first avoided disruption typically covers the entire program investment.

**Competitive positioning as a strategic platform company.** The parent conglomerate operates S/4HANA Cloud Private Edition. When CSS completes its migration, the group gains the ability to share data, consolidate reporting, and coordinate supply chain planning at the conglomerate level in ways that are architecturally unavailable today. For a satellite manufacturer competing for constellation contracts that require demonstrated supply chain resilience and financial transparency, operating on the same platform as your parent is not just an IT governance preference — it is a business capability.

**Estimated payback period:** 30 to 42 months from Wave 1 go-live, based on the financial close and revenue recognition savings alone. The supply chain and compliance risk value, incorporated in a full business case, would likely reduce this to 18 to 30 months. A formal business case should be developed during the Assessment and Scoping engagement using CSS's actual cost data.

### Cost of Inaction

The decision not to proceed is not cost-free. The ongoing cost of the current state includes:

- SAP extended maintenance premium post-2027: estimated 2 to 4 percent additional annual cost on the current ECC maintenance contract, with no new capability delivered
- Manual process labor costs for EVM, revenue recognition, BOM reconciliation, and close activities: estimated $1.5 million to $3 million annually, recurring
- Custom code maintenance burden: each of the 2,400 custom objects requires ongoing maintenance as CSS's business evolves, at an estimated loaded cost of $3,000 to $8,000 per object per year for active objects — a range of $3.6 million to $9.6 million annually against the full active estate
- ITAR compliance gap exposure: unquantified but material, as described above
- Opportunity cost of the digital factory vision: every quarter CSS operates on ECC is a quarter in which AI-assisted quality, predictive supply chain, and real-time manufacturing intelligence are architecturally unavailable — capabilities that peers who have completed their migrations are beginning to deploy

The total annual cost of inaction is estimated in the range of $6 million to $15 million, before compliance risk is incorporated. At the low end, the transformation program pays for itself in three to four years against this baseline. At the high end, it pays for itself in two years.

## 7. Why Now

CSS is at a moment where the forces driving this investment are converging rather than building slowly. Three of them are worth examining directly.

**The maintenance cliff is real and approaching.** SAP ECC extended maintenance ends in 2030. The December 2028 board mandate is not conservative — it is the minimum timeline that allows for a meaningful stabilization period before the extended maintenance window closes. If the program starts in mid-2026, Wave 1 go-live in Q4 2027 leaves thirteen months of stabilization before the board deadline. If the program start slips to 2027, the mathematics stop working.

**The production ramp creates a narrow implementation window.** The manufacturing organization's capacity to absorb a system change follows a curve: it is available now, before the ramp peaks, and it will be available again after the ramp stabilizes in 2027. The window for Wave 1 Finance scope is open today. The window for Wave 2 Manufacturing scope will open in 2027 and must be used before any subsequent operational challenge absorbs it. Waiting until after the ramp to begin Wave 1 does not create a more favorable environment — it simply wastes the implementation window that exists now.

**The compliance exposure does not wait for convenient timing.** The ITAR gaps your CISO has identified exist today. Every transaction touching an ITAR-controlled item that passes through the current fragmented control environment is an exposure event. Export compliance enforcement does not schedule itself around enterprise implementation programs.

**Quick wins available within Wave 1:**

- SAP Signavio process baseline workshops delivering documented current-state process maps within sixty days of engagement — a tangible deliverable visible to the whole organization before any system changes
- Custom code object classification report within six weeks of the SAP Readiness Check — giving the CIO the first credible answer to "what are we actually dealing with"
- GTS compliance gap remediation plan, accepted by the CISO, within the first Explore phase — the first formal step toward closing the compliance exposure
